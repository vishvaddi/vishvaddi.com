Italian luxury retail brands issue tender documents using a GC schedule
template that originates from Milan and assumes a European general-contractor
model. In that model, the GC carries scope items that, in Australian shopping
centre fitouts, typically sit with centre management or are excluded entirely.

When their schedule says **"under GC"** against an item, it does not
necessarily mean Australian centre management agrees. Always confirm the
actual split in writing with the centre tenancy delivery manager before
pricing.

## Items that frequently appear "under GC" in Italian retail tender docs

| Scope item | Italian convention | Australian shopping centre convention |
|---|---|---|
| Project / works insurance | Under GC | Often by tenant or centre — varies |
| Work permits and approvals | Under GC | AWP / PERCOW often by centre, building cert by GC |
| Hoarding (supply, install, removal) | Under GC | **Often by centre** at major malls |
| General public protection during fitout | Under GC | Often by centre |
| Drawing development (HQ, MEP, as-built) | Under GC, AUD $30–60k typical | Usually by client / consultant team |
| Centre approvals and hoarding signage | Under GC | Often by centre |

## Estimator's pre-mortem questions

Before issuing the quote:

1. Email the centre tenancy delivery manager and ask **in writing** which of
   the above are by centre versus by tenant. Get a written reply.
2. Don't assume. The same brand at two different centres will have different
   splits.
3. If the centre confirms hoarding is under tenant scope, allow $8–15k+
   depending on hoarding size and finish requirements.
4. If drawing development is under GC, allow at least a $50k PC SUM until you
   can get a firm quote from a draftsman or architect.

## Why this is recurring

The Italian HQ designs the brand experience and writes the spec once for
global rollout. They don't customise the scope split per country or per
centre — that is the local GC's problem. If you don't catch it at tender
stage, you absorb it.
